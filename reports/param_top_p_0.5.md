# Business Impact Assessment Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This report analyzes recent supply chain disruptions affecting TransGlobal Manufacturing, focusing on their impact on operations, financials, and overall business continuity. The assessment highlights key disruptions, root causes, and provides actionable mitigation strategies to enhance resilience against future risks.

## 2. Identified Disruptions
- **Natural Disasters**: Events in South Korea (tsunami, flooding) and Canada (earthquake).
- **Supplier Bankruptcy**: Incidents in Thailand and Brazil.
- **Quality Failures**: Multiple occurrences across South America, Europe, and North America.
- **Transport Disruptions**: Rail network failures in various regions, including North America and South America.
- **Labor Strikes**: Protests affecting operations in multiple countries.
- **Cyber Attacks**: Incidents in Brazil and the Netherlands.
- **Port Congestion**: Significant delays in Europe and Africa.

## 3. Root Causes
- **Natural Disasters**: Environmental factors such as tsunamis, flooding, and earthquakes.
- **Financial Issues**: Supplier bankruptcies due to cash-flow crises.
- **Quality Control Failures**: Defective batches leading to production downtimes.
- **Transport Failures**: Rail network failures and port congestion causing delays.
- **Labor Issues**: Protests related to working conditions.
- **Cybersecurity Breaches**: System vulnerabilities leading to operational disruptions.

## 4. Supplier Impact
- Tier 1 suppliers were primarily affected by natural disasters and quality failures, leading to significant production downtimes and increased costs.
- Tier 2 suppliers faced disruptions from bankruptcies and transport issues, impacting their ability to deliver critical components.
- Overall, 23 suppliers were affected across various tiers, leading to a ripple effect on production and logistics.

## 5. Inventory Impact
- Inventory levels fluctuated significantly, with some disruptions resulting in decreased days of inventory on hand (DOH), affecting the ability to meet production demands.
- Critical components, particularly aluminum and semiconductors, faced severe shortages, necessitating emergency inventory allocations.

## 6. Production Risk
- Production downtimes ranged from minimal (1 hour) to extensive (448 hours), particularly due to quality failures and natural disasters.
- Affected SKUs varied widely, with some disruptions impacting over 500 SKUs, indicating a broad risk to product availability.

## 7. Logistics Risk
- Increased logistics costs were noted across multiple disruptions, with some events leading to cost increases of over 70%.
- Transport mode changes and delays significantly impacted delivery timelines, affecting customer satisfaction and revenue.

## 8. Financial Implications
- Total revenue at risk across all disruptions exceeded $10 million, with financial impacts ranging from minor to over $4 million per event.
- The highest financial impacts were associated with quality failures and transport disruptions, necessitating urgent attention.

## 9. Recommended Mitigation Strategies
1. **Natural Disasters**:
   - **Strategy**: Enhance disaster recovery plans and invest in resilient infrastructure.
   - **Justification**: Reduces downtime and expedites recovery.

2. **Supplier Bankruptcy**:
   - **Strategy**: Diversify supplier base and establish financial health monitoring systems.
   - **Justification**: Minimizes dependency on single suppliers and identifies risks early.

3. **Quality Failures**:
   - **Strategy**: Implement 100% inspection
