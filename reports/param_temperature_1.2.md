# Business Impact Assessment Report

## 1. Executive Summary
Throughout January and February 2026, TransGlobal Manufacturing encountered numerous supply chain disruptions across various regions, predominantly in Asia and Europe. The events spanned natural disasters, quality failures, and labor strikes, negatively affecting operations, jeopardizing business continuity, and resulting in significant financial risks. This assessment identifies key disruptions and offers feasible mitigation strategies aimed at stabilizing operations and minimizing future impact.

## 2. Identified Disruptions
- **Natural Disasters:** Typhoons, flooding, and earthquakes affected multiple plants in Asia and North America.
- **Supplier Bankruptcy:** Two events noted in Thailand and Brazil led to operational delays.
- **Transport Disruptions:** Significant interruptions from rail network failures impacted multiple facilities.
- **Quality Failures:** High-severity failures severely impacted production lines in Germany and Argentina.
- **Labor Strikes:** Occurred in Ontario, USA, and throughout South America, implicating various operational sectors.

## 3. Root Causes
- **Natural Disasters:** Environmental challenges (tsunamis, flooding, earthquakes) leading to physical damage and loss.
- **Supplier Bankruptcy:** Financial instability among partners.
- **Quality Failures:** Defective materials prompting heightened production downtime.
- **Transport Disruptions:** Inadequacies in logistics and rail service affecting accessibility.
- **Labor Strikes:** Industrial disputes driven by working conditions issues.

## 4. Supplier Impact
- Various Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers have suffered varying levels of impact, from delayed shipments to complete bankruptcy.
- Notable issues arose with critical components (semiconductors, steel), essential to production.

## 5. Inventory Impact
- Disruptions generally led to decreased effective inventory on hand, affecting production capability.
- Extended downtime influenced inventory depletion rates, particularly after the flooding and quality failures.

## 6. Production Risk
- Production downtimes reached up to 448 hours, specifically in Germany due to quality failures, threatening operational capacity and service delivery.
- Risk levels were classified mostly as low to critical, requiring attention to various escalation scenarios.

## 7. Logistics Risk
- Increased logistics costs stemmed from expedited procurement measures taken during disruptions.
- Transportation delays averaged from 1 to 28 days, significantly impacting timely service.

- Total revenue at risk from selected disruptions approached $10 million, with specified financial impacts varying across incidents.
