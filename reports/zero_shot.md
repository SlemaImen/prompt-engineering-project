# Business Impact Assessment Report

## Executive Summary
This report analyzes a series of supply chain disruption events that occurred between January 1, 2026, and March 1, 2026. The disruptions were caused by various factors, including natural disasters, supplier bankruptcies, quality failures, transport disruptions, labor strikes, and cyber attacks. The assessment highlights the severity of these disruptions, their impact on suppliers, inventory, production, logistics, and financial implications, along with recommended mitigation strategies.

## Identified Disruptions
- **Total Events Analyzed**: 25
- **Types of Disruptions**:
  - Natural Disasters: 5
  - Supplier Bankruptcies: 2
  - Quality Failures: 6
  - Transport Disruptions: 6
  - Labor Strikes: 4
  - Cyber Attacks: 2
  - Customs Delays: 1
  - Port Congestion: 3

## Root Causes
- **Natural Disasters**: Tsunami, flooding, earthquake, typhoon.
- **Supplier Bankruptcies**: Cash-flow crises.
- **Quality Failures**: Defective batches, contamination, substandard materials.
- **Transport Disruptions**: Fuel shortages, rail network failures, container backlogs.
- **Labor Strikes**: Protests over working conditions.
- **Cyber Attacks**: System breaches and network outages.
- **Customs Delays**: Documentation errors.

## Supplier Impact
- **Tier 1 Suppliers**: Most affected by quality failures and natural disasters, leading to significant production downtimes and financial impacts.
- **Tier 2 Suppliers**: Experienced disruptions primarily due to bankruptcies and transport issues, affecting multiple SKUs.
- **Tier 3 Suppliers**: Less frequent disruptions but still impacted by quality failures and transport issues.

## Inventory Impact
- **Average Inventory Days on Hand (DOH) Before Disruptions**: 43
- **Average Inventory Days on Hand (DOH) After Disruptions**: 29
- **Significant Reductions**: Notable drops in inventory levels were observed, particularly during quality failures and transport disruptions.

## Production Risk
- **Total Production Downtime Hours**: 1,200+
- **Most Affected Facilities**: 
  - Berlin Manufacturing Plant (448 hours due to quality failure).
  - Texas Distribution Center (157 hours due to transport disruption).
- **Affected SKUs**: Ranged from 8 to 546 across various events.

## Logistics Risk
- **Increased Logistics Costs**: Ranged from 9% to 77% across events.
- **Transport Modes Affected**: Road, rail, sea, and air transport faced disruptions, leading to delays and increased costs.
- **Average Shipment Delay Days**: 10 days across events.

## Financial Implications
- **Total Revenue at Risk**: Approximately $12 million.
- **Total Financial Impact**: Approximately $7 million.
- **Critical Events**: 
  - EVT-010 (Berlin Manufacturing Plant) with a financial impact of $4.25 million.
  - EVT-014 (Morocco) with a financial impact of $3.42 million.

## Recommended Mitigation Strategies
- **Enhance Supplier Diversification**: Reduce reliance on single suppliers, especially for critical components.
- **Implement Robust Quality Control**: Increase inspection protocols to prevent quality failures.
- **Develop Contingency Plans**: Establish alternative transport routes and modes to mitigate transport disruptions.
- **Strengthen Cybersecurity Measures**: Protect against potential cyber threats through enhanced IT infrastructure.
- **Increase Inventory Buffers**: Maintain strategic inventory levels for critical components to cushion against disruptions.

## Business Continuity Assessment
- **Overall Status**: 
  - Several facilities are classified as "At Risk" or "Severely At Risk," particularly due to quality failures and transport disruptions.
  - Continuous monitoring and proactive measures are essential to stabilize operations and mitigate future risks.
- **Recommended Actions**: Regularly review and update business continuity plans, focusing on high-risk areas identified in this assessment.
