# Business Impact Assessment Report

## Executive Summary
This report provides a comprehensive assessment of the recent supply chain disruptions impacting TransGlobal Manufacturing. Key disruptions include natural disasters, supplier bankruptcies, transport issues, quality failures, and labor strikes. The financial implications are significant, with potential revenue at risk across multiple facilities. This assessment outlines the root causes of disruptions, their effects on supplier relationships, inventory levels, production capabilities, logistics, and overall financial health. It concludes with tailored mitigation strategies to enhance resilience and ensure business continuity.

## Identified Disruptions
- **Natural Disasters**: Events in South Korea (tsunami, flooding) and Canada (earthquake).
- **Supplier Bankruptcies**: Notable cases in Thailand and Brazil.
- **Transport Disruptions**: Rail network failures in multiple regions, including North America and Brazil.
- **Quality Failures**: Defective batches affecting steel, plastics, and copper.
- **Labor Strikes**: Protests affecting production in various regions including the USA, Canada, and South America.

## Root Causes
- **Natural Disasters**: Environmental phenomena leading to production and shipment delays.
- **Supplier Bankruptcies**: Financial instability and cash-flow crises affecting Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers.
- **Transport Disruptions**: Infrastructure failures, including fuel shortages and rail network issues.
- **Quality Failures**: Issues with material integrity and inspection processes.
- **Labor Strikes**: Poor working conditions and union disputes.

## Supplier Impact
- Disruptions have primarily affected Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers, with operational impacts leading to delays in critical materials such as aluminum, semiconductors, and steel.
- The financial strain on suppliers, particularly those facing bankruptcy, necessitates a review of supplier diversification strategies.

## Inventory Impact
- Fluctuations in inventory days on hand (DOH) were noted, with several disruptions leading to decreased inventory levels, thus increasing the risk of stockouts.
- Events like the quality failure in Argentina resulted in significant inventory reductions, impacting the ability to meet production demands.

## Production Risk
- Production downtimes ranged from a few hours to over 448 hours in critical events, leading to substantial operational disruptions.
- High affected SKU counts in events indicate systemic risks that could disrupt overall manufacturing capabilities.
