# Business Impact Assessment Report

## Executive Summary
This report analyzes a series of supply chain disruptions that occurred between January 1, 2026, and March 1, 2026. The disruptions were categorized by type, severity, and impact on operations, inventory, and finances. Key findings indicate a mix of natural disasters, quality failures, transport disruptions, and labor strikes, with varying degrees of impact on business continuity.

## Identified Disruptions
- **Total Events Analyzed**: 25
- **Types of Disruptions**:
  - Natural Disasters: 6
  - Quality Failures: 6
  - Transport Disruptions: 6
  - Labor Strikes: 4
  - Supplier Bankruptcies: 2
  - Port Congestion: 2
  - Cyber Attacks: 2
  - Customs Delays: 1

## Root Causes
- **Natural Disasters**: Tsunami, flooding, earthquake, typhoon.
- **Quality Failures**: Defective batches, contamination, substandard materials.
- **Transport Disruptions**: Fuel shortages, rail network failures, container backlogs.
- **Labor Strikes**: Protests over working conditions.
- **Supplier Bankruptcies**: Cash-flow crises.
- **Cyber Attacks**: System breaches and network outages.
- **Customs Delays**: Documentation errors.

## Supplier Impact
- **Tier 1 Suppliers**: Most affected by quality failures and natural disasters, leading to significant production downtimes.
- **Tier 2 Suppliers**: Experienced disruptions primarily due to bankruptcies and transport issues.
- **Tier 3 Suppliers**: Impacted by quality failures and cyber attacks, affecting lower-tier components.

## Inventory Impact
- **Average Inventory Days on Hand (DOH)**: 
  - Before disruptions: 43 days
  - After disruptions: 29 days
- **Significant Reductions**: Notable drops in inventory levels post-disruption, particularly in events with high severity.
- **Affected SKUs**: Ranged from 8 to 546 across events, indicating widespread impact on product availability.

## Production Risk
- **Total Production Downtime**: 1,080 hours across all events.
- **High Downtime Events**: 
  - EVT-010 (Berlin Manufacturing Plant): 448 hours due to critical quality failure.
  - EVT-005 (Texas Distribution Center): 157 hours due to transport disruption.
- **Affected Facilities**: Multiple facilities across North America, Europe, and Asia faced significant production interruptions.

## Logistics Risk
- **Increased Logistics Costs**: Ranged from 9% to 77% across events.
- **Transport Modes Affected**: Rail and sea transport faced the most disruptions, leading to increased costs and delays.
- **Average Lead Time Increase**: 10 days across events, with some events causing lead time increases of up to 27 days.

## Financial Implications
- **Total Revenue at Risk**: Approximately $12.5 million across all events.
- **Financial Impact**: Total financial losses estimated at $7.5 million.
- **High-Impact Events**: 
  - EVT-010: $4.2 million loss.
  - EVT-004: $1.0 million loss.
  - EVT-012: $840,000 loss.

## Recommended Mitigation Strategies
- **Emergency Procurement**: For critical components, especially from Tier 1 suppliers.
- **Inventory Allocation**: Implement emergency inventory allocation strategies for high-risk materials.
- **Cross-Training Employees**: To mitigate labor strike impacts and ensure operational continuity.
- **Diversification of Suppliers**: Reduce reliance on single suppliers, particularly in high-risk regions.
- **Enhanced Quality Control**: Implement 100% inspections for critical components to prevent quality failures.

## Business Continuity Assessment
- **Overall Status**: 
  - 5 events classified as "Severely At Risk."
  - 6 events classified as "At Risk."
  - 14 events classified as "Stable with Monitoring."
- **Recommendations for Business Continuity**: Continuous monitoring of high-risk suppliers and regions, regular updates to disaster recovery plans, and investment in technology to enhance supply chain visibility and resilience.

This assessment highlights the need for proactive risk management strategies to mitigate the impact of future disruptions on the supply chain.
